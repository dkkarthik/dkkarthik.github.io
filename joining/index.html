Joining my lab

Prospective PhD students

I am always looking for students with a strong aptitude for research and a solid technical background in robotics. Given the current research ecosystem in the US, I am only likely to take on students who already hold a Master’s degree in a relevant field. You should also have a demonstrated research record in robotics, ideally including one or more peer-reviewed papers at robotics conferences or journals. If this describes you, please send me a short email with specifics about your research work and background.

A note on cold emails: I receive several emails a day along the lines of “I read your paper X and was really inspired. I did Y (an unrelated activity) and think I fit your research.” I do not respond to such emails. If you have not spent a few hours reading my relevant papers and cannot show why you are a good fit for ongoing work in my lab, I will not spend the ten minutes it takes to respond. Please save yourself some time: narrow down the faculty you genuinely want to work with, write to them yourself, and do not send generic, AI-written messages that no one will read.

As a reference, I consider the publication venues listed on roboranking.org as relevant venues for robotics. To that list, I would add Autonomous Robots, ICLR, and the IFRR conferences as relevant robotics venues as well.
Papers that are in submission or published at venues that I don’t recognize are the same as not having published previously.

Prospective Masters students

If you are a prospective Masters student applying to UB, please apply through the university’s application portals. I particularly encourage you to consider the MS in Robotics program, which I co-direct. We do not have assistantships for incoming MS students.

Each semester, I host between 5 and 10 MS students for voluntary research across several projects. Based on their interest and performance, some of them go on to do a Masters thesis with me on their research topic. Several students have also co-authored peer-reviewed papers in top robotics conferences and journals based on their work in my lab. The best way to get into my lab is to take my class, CSE 568, and do well in it. I also consider students who have taken Computer Vision, Machine Learning, or robotics-related courses in MAE or ISE; if you have a related background, that is something we can discuss once you are at UB. Each semester, please look for emails from me advertising lab projects that are looking for help. Please do not email me before you come to UB — I cannot respond beyond what I have said here.
