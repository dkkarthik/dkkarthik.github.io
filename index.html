Research

My work focuses on robot perception and autonomy for real-world operation: systems that can perceive reliably, act safely, and survive contact with actual deployment constraints instead of just looking good in a paper figure.

Robot Perception

Task-specific representations, visual sensing, scene understanding, and robust inference for robots working in messy environments.

Safe Autonomy

Planning and decision-making under uncertainty, with an emphasis on dependable behavior outside carefully controlled settings.

Real-World Deployment

Field robotics for excavation, inspection, and other operational settings where perception and autonomy have to actually hold up.

Featured Projects

Flagship efforts that reflect the lab's broader agenda in robust perception, safe autonomy, and real-world deployment.

Excavation Autonomy

Real-world excavation is one of the current application areas driving the lab's work in task-aware perception, robust visual sensing, and safe autonomous operation under field conditions.

Project

Culvert Inspection

Culvert inspection is the second major application theme currently highlighted in the lab: a good example of autonomy and perception designed for infrastructure settings rather than clean indoor demos.

Project

GPU-Accelerated Visual SLAM

A cluster of recent work focuses on systems challenges in visual SLAM, including GPU acceleration for tracking, bundle adjustment, and loop-closure related computation.
